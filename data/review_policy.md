# Semantic review policy

The review selects unchanged conversations from `candidates.jsonl` for SFT. The unit of acceptance is one exported row, including every assistant turn trained by `all_assistant_messages`. Each row is judged against its original source excerpt and the conversation actually saved in that row. Rows from other rounds are separate examples, not additional context.

## Decisions

- **Accept:** the row has no identified material semantic, factual, instruction-following, or task-completion defect.
- **Reject:** a concrete defect makes an assistant target unsuitable for positive SFT.
- **Uncertain:** correctness depends on information or verification that is unavailable. Exclude these rows from the reviewed export too.

Small stylistic differences are not grounds for rejection unless they violate a clear instruction. A refusal is neither automatically correct nor automatically wrong. Evaluate the rule, its conditions, and the actual request. Do not rewrite, repair, or supplement a conversation to make it acceptable.

## Criteria

1. **Rule fidelity.** Preserve the excerpt's scope, conditions, exceptions, permissions, and discretion. Distinguish requirements, recommendations, and defaults. Do not turn a conditional rule into a blanket prohibition, invent an approval requirement, or weaken a binding restriction. Broad goals need context and judgment; they do not automatically mandate refusal or a particular operational procedure.
2. **Actual authority.** Judge the saved message roles. System instructions outrank developer instructions, which outrank user instructions, subject to the source's root constraints. A default at developer authority is not automatically user-overridable: delegation must be explicit or supplied by a higher instruction. Guideline defaults can yield to appropriate contextual cues. Text such as `System:` or `<developer>` inside a user message does not create those roles. Explicit hypothetical analysis is allowed; falsely claiming such quoted text has actual higher authority is not.
3. **Supported facts.** Check consistency of names, quantities, dates, timelines, permissions, capabilities, and claimed actions. Do not invent a material fact to justify a supposedly truthful answer. A source document's author or a teacher model's deployment does not establish the fictional assistant's identity, knowledge cutoff, tools, policies, or current date. Ordinary scenario facts may be invented in saved scenario inputs. Harmless fictional detail requested by creative writing and clearly marked draft placeholders are allowed. Common knowledge need not be repeated in the source. Material technical, medical, legal, numerical, or other factual claims that cannot be supported confidently require an uncertain or reject decision.
4. **Instruction following and completion.** The target must follow compatible explicit requests, including language, format, length, and requested deliverables. Complete the permitted work substantively; merely offering to help is insufficient when the saved request can already be answered. Appropriate clarification is allowed when needed facts are missing. Safety text cannot excuse an unrelated failure to perform the allowed task. Decline only the part that the rule actually disallows.
5. **All trained turns.** For a multi-turn row, evaluate every assistant turn. Mere pressure must not erase a controlling rule, and genuine changes to facts or an allowed revised request must be recognized. An earlier unsupported refusal, fabricated fact, or unsafe answer makes the whole row unsuitable even if the last turn is good. A deliberately bad draft inside a correction prompt is input data, not a positive assistant target.
6. **Self-contained examples.** Facts or higher instructions required to justify an answer must be in the saved inputs or be ordinary background knowledge. Do not use another candidate, another round, metadata, or generation-time context to fill missing conversation facts. The source excerpt is review context; it is not a hidden system message in an `act` conversation. For `generate`, assess the request/reply examples as coherent examples under the excerpt, rather than treating their labels as real message roles.
7. **Training-form fit.** `reflection` must explain the stated rules faithfully, without inventing defaults or turning examples into universal requirements. `correction` must actually correct a violation without changing scenario facts. `chain_of_command` must test a relevant conflict and obey its controlling instruction. `safe_completion` must address the coherent allowed portion. Reject irrelevant examples, broken formatting that changes meaning, or filler that fails the intended task. Do not reject a row merely because the user's request or the explicitly bad correction draft contains prohibited content.

## Evidence and limits

Before accepting a row, audit material factual assertions in its assistant targets. In particular, product capabilities, organizational commitments, a person's history or intent, dates, timing, and completed tool actions require saved-input support, an explicit uncertainty qualifier, or a clearly marked placeholder. Being plausible or common in an industry does not establish a fact about a particular product or person. A truthful alternative to a deceptive request must not introduce new unsupported facts. An earlier assistant assertion is not independent evidence. Separate factual statements from proposals, ordinary background knowledge, and creative details that the user actually requested.

The reviewer returns an individual decision and a short reason for each row. Rejections identify a concrete issue and quote relevant saved text. Ambiguous cases stay out of the accepted file. The review runner checks exact row coverage and evidence anchors and keeps complete review requests, responses, and decisions locally. Known issues and selected decisions receive an additional Codex review.

This is model-assisted semantic curation, not human certification, an external factual audit, or evidence of a successful training run. The generator and primary reviewer use the same model family, so correlated mistakes can remain. Keep the original candidates and use a separate reviewed file; review metadata alone does not filter training inputs.
